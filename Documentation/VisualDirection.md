# Visual Direction


## feedback on Levi's work so far - Friday 2nd Oct

Heyo, I realised after writing this that it's a lot! And that's not because I don't think this is great work, but there's lots to unpack and I realised through writing all this how important the visual communication is to get our themes across! So just bringing it back to some core visual concepts from our first few meetings:
- **Symbiopunk:** no inorganic technology.
- **Integration/cyborgism:** we see a spectrum of humans with different relationships with their shells, with meaningful  proportions between Human-Shell. 
- **Not mermaids:** these humans are still mammals best suited for land, and their symbiotic technology is overall a bit awkward and un-ergonomic.

### Shell-culuture
I think this can be a rough guide and I'm keen to see how you develop this more:
- Deeper = bigger and spikier shells! More maximalist, more antennae, customisewd to each personality.
- Medium = less variety in shells; uniform and "production-line". Human-shell proportions are closer to equal, but they still fit totally inside of them.
- Shallow = Smoother shells; less antennae. These folks are mostly offline, and much bigger than their shells if they have them. They're treated more like optional accessories: fannypacks; shoulder-bags. Nomadic, not tied to a particular shell.


### Character designs
- House-size-shell proportions are good for deep-sea; but think of it more like a cramped submarine than a suburban house. 
- Overall, we wanna have people be a lot more dwarfed by their shells: they live in them most of the time! 
- The person with the super-heavy backpack is fantastic, it's like they're trying to pull themselves out of the Depths and fighting against technology that was designed to keep them down there. Definitely the kind of dynamic for our Protagonist.
- The Backpack page + the person living in a shell in a cramped fetal-position shell is really gooood, it conveys how they might not have been built for such depths, but rather to be more hybrid, but they're not used as intended. This could be our protagonist. Try a few different proportions in this range -- how would they fit inside each shell?
- I like the texture + colour sketches of the clothes a lot, frills and ponchos feel very fitting. I would be keen to hear some ideas from you on what materials they might be making them from? Just so it doesn't get ambiguously mer-maidy.
- These undersea folks would all generally wear heavier clothes, like westuits, for protection from UV, pressure, and cold.
- Don't dull down those super bold shell silhouettes from those initial sketches -- they're great!!
- Give the spikes on shells an antennae-look like rooftop TV aerials, satellites or radios.
- Could Moon Cycles fit into the visual personalities of these folks?
