
# Games that InterTidal takes inspiration from

### Kara Stone (2019) Ritual of The Moon

- Kara Stone has said she is interested in **exploring “how humans experience time” through the mundane beauty of routine and ritual.**
- The slow play of this really impacted me, and reminded me of a lack of ritual in our modern lives that I was sorely missing.
- About 10 different endings through subtle choices.
- **Takes place over a lunar cycle** (but I think it's asynchronous)
- Requires you to play only once a day, for five minutes, to progress the story.
- Dealing with impending cataclysm and giving you an **everyday choice between action and inaction**

### Kenny Sun (2021) *Tender: Creature Comforts*

!image.png

- A queer dating-app game!
- Interesting dialogue system where you select from “shorthand” options and then fake-type them your keyboard (very juicy)
- You can slightly personalise your responses with different typing styles
- Schedule dates for real times (e.g. Friday 2nd Oct, 6pm) - you can miss them!
- There are sometimes "fakeout" dialogue options that aren't consistent with what you intended to type and reveal your anxieties or your masking: emulating the **room for rumination that is inherent online socialisation**
- Subtle worldbuilding through casual conversation
- Characters aren’t just there at the service of the player: they might ghost you.
- Characters have active hours: they’re sensitive to your timing of messages.

### Porpentine (2019) *With Those We Love Alive*

!image.png

- Text adventure with limited choices, which can start to feel like proceduralised depression.
- Blurs the line between the protagonist and the player through asking them to draw sigils on their body.
- Within repetitive choices could choose to construct a daily routine for yourself. There are occasional little “glimmers” of novelty if you do decide to taking a daily walk.
- Second-person tense of writing: instructional, degrading, confronting.

### Superbrothers (2011) *Swords and Sworcery: EP*

!image.png

- An archetypal self-aware “hero’s journey” zelda-like that was an early hit for iOS.
- Made an impact in its use of the real-time lunar cycle, but was also easy to cheat.
- You are asked to be attentive to the environments' **subtle changes with the lunar cycles.**
- Explore the world mindfully, tapping around little **diorama-like scenes**
- Major narrative points happen at Dark-Moon/Bright-Moon, and missable smaller details around quarter-moons.

### ZA/UM (2019) *Disco Elysium*

!image.png

- Considering the critical success of this (and e.g. NORCO,  there's been a lot more of an interest in indie text-adventure games in the last decade.
- We're particularly interested in how *Disco* treats time: **time moves only when you talk to people**, so a lot of your **real-time can spent largely alone**.
- My first experience of *Disco Elysium* was lots of missed connections and unresolved feelings because I didn't take this finitude of time seriously.
- An “absurd” hero, and a confronting handling of mental health.
